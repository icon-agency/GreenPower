    How this backlog is organised

    What an epic is

    A container for work too big to be one ticket. It is not a bigger ticket — nobody is ever assigned an epic. It groups tickets so you can talk about progress without listing fourteen things.

    Epic  Content components   └─ Story  C2 Card and multi-card section       └─ Subtask  Build the field definitions

    The epic is the goal. The story is one shippable piece. The subtask is one person, one sitting.

    The story is the useful unit: one person can finish it, and someone else can verify it is done. That is why this backlog has of them across epics rather than a handful of fat ones — “build all the components” cannot be reviewed, but “Card renders with an optional image and pins its links to the foot” can.

    Put simply: the epic holds the why, the story holds the what. Any single ticket inside Foundations looks like plumbing on its own; the epic is what says nothing else lands cleanly until it is done.

    Why these epics

    Grouped by what blocks what, not by how the design system is arranged:

    • Foundations and Global regions go first — everything imports them.
    • Content components, Listings and Forms can run in parallel once foundations land.
    • Buy tool logic needs the directory to exist first.
    • Page templates come last: they are assembly, not new code.
    • Decisions are not dev work at all.

    Why decisions get their own epic

    A decision buried inside a build ticket is invisible until a developer hits it mid-sprint. Pulled out, D3 (“can households buy gas?”) visibly blocks L2, and it can be chased a week early. Keep these out of a sprint until they are answered.
